# Mirror-Mirror 
## Synopsis

Set in present-day rural Egypt, **MIRROR MIRROR** is an intimate humanitarian drama with a restrained magical-realism core. The film tells the story of **Maha**, a ten-year-old girl caught between the harsh economic reality of child agricultural labor and the gentle, dignified childhood she was denied.

### The Story

* **The Reflection**: At dawn, in a deteriorating one-room home, ten-year-old Maha washes her face and looks into an old wooden-framed mirror. Looking back at her is an identical reflection wearing a clean sunshine-yellow cotton dress and a red hair ribbon—smiling with the quiet ease of a child who feels safe. When their fingertips touch the glass, Maha steps across into the mirror-world.
* **The Childhood She Deserves**: Across the mirror line, life is ordinary, warm, and safe. Maha sits down to a peaceful breakfast with her mother and father, walks to school with neighborhood friends, raises her hand eagerly in class, laughs after a stumble in the courtyard, and falls asleep at night after her mother kisses her forehead.
* **The Other Side**: A match cut breaks the reverie. Maha opens her eyes back in reality—wearing her faded beige tunic and worn sandals—summoned by her mother's urgent voice. Instead of a school backpack, she climbs into the open bed of a dusty agricultural pickup truck, watching a passing school bus full of laughing children fade into the distance.
* **The Field**: Beneath a punishing midday sun, Maha spends hours bending across dry peanut furrows and dragging heavy sacks under the watchful eye of an impatient supervisor. A fleeting heat-shimmer vision of her yellow-dress self holding a schoolbook offers a moment of longing before dissolving back into dust.
* **The Journey Home & Aftermath**: At sunset, the exhausted children pile into the truck bed for the ride home. As dusk settles, approaching vehicle headlights reflect in Maha’s alarmed eyes. The screen cuts abruptly to black—sparing graphic collision imagery—leaving only the chilling silence of an off-screen accident, punctuated by a lone worn sandal, a broken pencil, and a red ribbon resting in the roadside dust.
* **The Last Reflection**: That night, Maha's mother sits alone in the dark room clutching her daughter's folded clothing. Drawn to the mirror, she sees Mirror Maha one last time, holding her schoolbook. When the mother touches the glass, a hairline crack fractures the reflection until it fades into darkness—closing on the faint echo of a school bell and a child's voice: *"Teacher, I'm finished."*

> **"Every child deserves a childhood.**  
> **Sometimes poverty doesn't steal a child's future—it steals the childhood that was supposed to build it."**
