# Mirror-Mirror 
## Story Narration

> *A cinematic story of two childhoods: one denied, one deserved.*

### The Reflection
Before sunrise in a rural Egyptian village, ten-year-old Maha wakes on a thin mattress surrounded by cracked plaster walls. Beside her lies a torn notebook—her only link to the idea of school. Splashing water on her face, she looks into a worn wooden-framed mirror. Looking back is an impossible reflection: Maha in a clean sunshine-yellow dress, her hair neat and tied with a red ribbon, smiling with radiant health. When their fingertips meet against the glass, the mirror opens.

### The Childhood She Deserves
Across the threshold, the room transforms into a warm, safe home. A gentle breakfast with her mother and father fills the morning with laughter and care. Walking along the village road, Maha carries a schoolbag instead of a burlap sack, running alongside her friends. In the classroom, she raises her hand proudly to answer; in the courtyard, she stumbles and laughs as her classmates help her up. That evening, her mother gently checks her homework and kisses her forehead before tucking her into a real bed. She is safe, cherished, and free to simply be a child.

### The Other Side
Her mother’s urgent shout shatters the dream. Maha opens her eyes to the dim reality of the bare room, her yellow dress replaced by her dusty tunic. Stepping outside, she climbs into the crowded bed of an old agricultural pickup truck. As they pull onto the road, a school bus passes in the opposite direction. Maha watches the laughing schoolchildren through the windows until the bus disappears into the morning haze.

### The Field
Beneath the relentless midday sun, Maha kneels in the dry dirt, digging through furrows and straining to drag heavy sacks of harvested peanuts under the supervisor’s sharp shouts. Exhausted and parched, she looks toward the shimmering horizon and catches a brief glimpse of her mirror-self—smiling and holding a schoolbook—before the vision dissolves back into dust and heat.

### The Journey Home
At sunset, the truck carries the exhausted children home. In the fading light, a violent vibration rattles the rear wheel just as approaching headlights flood Maha's alarmed eyes. The screen cuts hard to black with the sound of impact and shattering glass. Without showing the collision, the quiet aftermath speaks for the tragedy: a lone worn sandal, a broken pencil, and a red ribbon lying in the roadside dust as an ambulance siren fades away.

### The Last Reflection
In the quiet of the night, Maha's mother sits in grief, clutching her daughter’s folded clothes. Drawn to the mirror, she sees Maha standing on the other side in her yellow dress, holding her schoolbook—no longer smiling, but looking directly at her mother. As their hands meet against the glass, delicate fractures spider across the surface. The reflection shatters into fragments and slowly disappears into the dark.

Over the black screen, distant playground laughter and a school bell echo with a quiet child's voice:  
*"Teacher, I'm finished."*
